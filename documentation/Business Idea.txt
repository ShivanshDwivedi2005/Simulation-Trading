SIMTRADE — BUSINESS IDEA
Version: 0.1

1. Problem
New and intermediate traders need realistic practice without financial loss. Many paper-trading products either hide execution mechanics, depend on broker paper accounts, or provide an experience that is too simplified for risk, strategy, and compliance training.

2. Solution
SimTrade is a US-market simulation terminal that uses a shared live-market data source for both charts and executions. Users receive virtual capital, submit orders to an internal simulation engine, see portfolio P&L update, and learn how spreads, slippage, risk limits, and order behavior affect outcomes.

3. Initial customer
- Self-directed learners preparing to trade US equities.
- Trading educators and cohorts that need consistent exercises.
- Universities and training teams teaching market microstructure, risk, or compliance.
- Advanced users who want to rehearse order and multi-leg workflows without broker exposure.

4. Differentiation
- The platform owns the simulation engine rather than forwarding orders to a broker paper account.
- One normalized quote stream drives both displayed prices and fills.
- Progressive realism: bid/ask fills first; liquidity, partial fills, market impact, and latency later.
- Configurable risk and non-accusatory compliance alerts make the product suitable for training.
- A provider abstraction supports future asset classes and regions without replacing the OMS.

5. Business model
- Free individual tier: delayed or basic feed, core order types, one simulated account.
- Pro tier: richer data, advanced analytics, strategies, exports, and configurable simulations.
- Education/team tier: cohorts, assignments, leaderboards, instructor controls, and audit exports.
- Enterprise training tier: SSO, custom rules, private instruments, support, and reporting.

6. North-star outcome
Verified learning sessions in which a user completes a trade, reviews the execution and P&L impact, and returns to practice again.

7. Guardrails
- Clearly label all activity as simulation; never imply that an order reaches a real exchange.
- Do not present simulated performance as a promise of real-world results.
- Treat market-data entitlements, exchange attribution, and redistribution rules as product requirements.
- Compliance alerts are educational surveillance signals, not findings of illegal activity.

8. Initial validation plan
- Recruit 10–20 active learners and 3–5 educators.
- Test whether the AAPL buy-to-P&L flow can be completed unaided in under three minutes.
- Measure order completion, chart-to-order conversion, repeat sessions, and error recovery.
- Interview users about realism, trust, and the next simulation control they would pay for.
